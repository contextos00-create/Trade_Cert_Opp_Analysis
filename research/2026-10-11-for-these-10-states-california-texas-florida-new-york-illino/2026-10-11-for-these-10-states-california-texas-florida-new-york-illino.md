# Plumber Licensing Authorities, Examinations, and Code Standards

Plumbing licensure across the targeted states is divided between state-level regulatory agencies and local municipal oversight, with testing delivered via specialized vendors such as PSI, Pearson VUE, or direct agency administration [1][2][3][5][10][12]. Plumbing examinations nationwide generally align with either the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) developed by IAPMO or the International Plumbing Code (IPC) standard [2][9][15][16][20]. While detailed frameworks are documented for California, Texas, Florida, and Washington, complete testing specifications remain uncollected for Illinois, Pennsylvania, Ohio, Georgia, Arizona, and New York [1][2][3][10][12].

---

## State Licensing Authorities, Testing Vendors, and Exam Structures

### California
* **Licensing Authority:** Contractors State License Board (CSLB) [1].
* **Testing Format & Guidelines:** CSLB publishes a candidate study guide for the C-36 Plumbing contractor classification [1]. Examinations scheduled on or after March 1, 2025, are closed-book, multiple-choice tests with no reference materials permitted during testing [20].
* **Code Standard:** The examination resource curriculum aligns with the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) via the 2025 California Plumbing Code (California Code of Regulations, Title 24, published by IAPMO) [9][20].
* **Content Outline & Weights:** The C-36 exam is divided into five sections [20]:
  * Planning and Estimating: 22% [20]
  * Underground and Rough Systems: 22% [20]
  * Finish Plumbing Installations: 20% [20]
  * Service, Repair, and Remodel Plumbing Systems: 19% [20]
  * Safety: 17% [20]

### Texas
* **Licensing & Administration:** Plumbing examinations are scheduled and taken through PSI testing centers [2].
* **Testing Format:** All Texas plumbing exams are closed book [2]. Texas provides specific preparation paths for Master Plumbers [9].
  * *Master Plumbing Exam:* 220 questions, 360-minute limit, 70% passing threshold [2].
  * *Tradesman Plumbing Exam:* Consists of a written section (90 questions, 120 minutes) and a practical section (6 questions, 240 minutes), both requiring a 70% passing score [2].
* **Recommended Code Study:** Recommends studying either the 2018 International Plumbing Code (IPC) and 2018 International Fuel Gas Code (IFGC), or the 2018 Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) [2].

### Florida
* **Licensing Authority & Administration:** Administered by the Department of Business and Professional Regulation (DBPR) for the Construction Industry Licensing Board (CILB) [3]. DBPR contracts with Professional Testing, Inc. to register candidates, develop, and score examinations, while Pearson VUE delivers computer-based testing [3].
* **Testing Delivery & Sessions:**
  * Candidates must take a Business & Finance exam (120 scored questions, 6.5 hours CBT via Pearson VUE) and a trade knowledge exam [3][18].
  * The Plumbing trade examination is a paper-and-pencil test administered in Orlando across dedicated test months [3][18]. It features an AM session (4.5 hours, 45 scored questions plus 5 isometric drawings) and a PM session (4.5 hours, 65 scored questions) [18].
* **Reference Materials:** Florida construction examinations are open book [3]. DBPR issues dedicated Candidate Information Booklets (CIB) for plumbing contractors and maintains approved reference lists, prohibiting non-approved materials [6][7].

### Washington
* **Licensing Authority & Vendor:** Regulated by the Washington State Department of Labor & Industries (L&I) Plumber Certification Program, with examinations proctored by PSI [11][12][13][15]. Journey level plumbers are designated as PL01 and require at least 4 years (8,000 hours) of trainee experience [11][14].
* **Code Standard:** Exams are based on the 2015 Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC) with amendments adopted by the Washington State Building Code Council [9][15].
* **Format & Allowed References:** Closed book, requiring a 70% passing score [12][15]. Outside reference materials are prohibited, but the exam provides specific UPC tables (Tables 702.1, 610.3, 610.4, and 610.10; Table 703.2 is not provided) [12][15].
* **Content Outline:** Three timed sections [12][15]:
  * *Exam One:* Technical and general trade knowledge covering UPC Chapters 1–10, 15, 16, and 17 (100 questions, 90 minutes) [12][15].
  * *Exam Two:* Waste and vent sizing/drawings based on Chapter 7 (25 questions, 90 minutes) [12][15].
  * *Exam Three:* Water piping sizing/drawings based on Chapter 6 (25 questions, 90 minutes) [12][15].

### Illinois
* **Licensing Authority:** The Illinois Department of Public Health (IDPH) regulates and licenses plumbers, contractors, and apprentices under the Illinois Plumbing License Law (225 ILCS 320) [10]. IDPH directly administers the state licensing exam sessions (e.g., at union training facilities) following an apprenticeship of 48 to 72 months [5][10].

### New York
* Plumbing training and examination preparation align with the International Plumbing Code (IPC) standards [9].

## National Code-Writing Bodies and Examination Vendors

* **IAPMO (International Association of Plumbing and Mechanical Officials):** Authors and publishes the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), which is designated as an American National Standard by ANSI [16]. IAPMO also publishes the California Plumbing Code (CCR Title 24) [20].
* **ICC (International Code Council):** Develops codes, standards, and building safety solutions globally [17][19]. ICC publishes the California Building Code [20]. Pearson VUE administers ICC Contractor/Trades certification exams, publishing exam information bulletins and outlines across participating jurisdictions [4].
* **National Testing Providers:** PSI and Pearson VUE (transitioning its brand name to Pearson Professional Assessments) administer trade and contractor credentialing exams across state programs [2][4][8][12][15].

## What is Still Uncertain or Missing

* **Development of the IPC:** While the International Code Council (ICC) develops building safety codes and standards [17][19], and New York/Texas recognize the International Plumbing Code (IPC) [2][9], the findings do not explicitly confirm that ICC authors and develops the IPC.
* **Missing State Jurisdictions:** Licensing authorities, testing vendors, and candidate information bulletins are undocumented for Ohio (OCILB), Georgia, and Arizona (ROC).
* **Local Jurisdictions:** Specific municipal regulatory bodies, examination vendors, and candidate bulletins for New York City, Philadelphia, Pittsburgh, and Allegheny County remain unidentified.
* **Exam Details for Incomplete States:** Illinois testing vendor details, exam outlines, adopted code editions, and pass criteria were not established beyond IDPH's statutory oversight [5][10].
* **Curriculum Weights & Approved Booklists:** Topic percentage breakdowns are missing for Florida, Texas, and Illinois trade exams, and Florida’s exact list of approved open-book reference titles was not retrieved.
* **Role of NASCLA:** The role of the National Association of State Contractors Licensing Agencies (NASCLA) in plumbing examination development across these states was not documented.
